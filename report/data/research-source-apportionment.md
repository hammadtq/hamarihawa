## Seasonal drivers
Lahore: PM2.5 peaks Nov-Jan (Urban Unit Lahore EI, CAMS 2011-2021 monthly data). Frontiers 2023 "fifth season" paper: during episodes boundary layer collapses to 296-330 m, winds 0-3 mph vs 8.5 mph on clear days, RH >75% (88% mornings) so smoke nucleates fog. Springer/ESPR 2026 IGP-Lahore paper: persistent inversions at 700-900 hPa, weak surface winds, RH 80-95% under anticyclonic stagnation, coinciding with a tripling of regional emissions (REAS v3.2); CMIP6 SSP5-8.5 projects more frequent stagnation windows 2025-2049 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42141223/). Rice-residue burning is confined to Oct-Nov (FAO), which is why FAO calls agriculture "significant to seasonal smog although third by emissions". Lodhi 2009 measured winter PM2.5 ~4x other seasons with high secondary sulfate in fog. Islamabad: basin ringed by the Margalla Hills; thermal inversions in autumn/spring trap pollutants; suburban biomass heating adds winter PM2.5 (J. Trace Elements paper); worst month on record Dec 2024.

## Level of disagreement
1. Metric mismatch explains most of the spread. Total-mass inventories (dominated by CO) give transport 83% (Lahore) or 39-43% (Punjab). PM2.5-only inventories give transport 35% (Urban Unit Lahore, PAQI) or just 7% (Urban Unit Punjab, where industry 34% + NCIP 30% dominate). Ambient-concentration modelling (World Bank) puts residential solid fuel first (23-28%) and transport second (16-24%), a sector both Urban Unit inventories put near zero (0.11%).
2. Crop and waste burning: 3.9%/3.6% of total mass but 33%/29% of PM2.5 in the same Urban Unit Lahore inventory. PAQI does not separate crop burning at all.
3. Brick kilns: only PAQI separates them (17% Lahore, 18% Islamabad); WB folds them into industry; a 2025 dispersion study suggests 20-30% locally near kiln belts.
4. India's share for Lahore: 13% (WB, Punjab-wide), 17% of point sources (FAO), 30% (Punjab govt, unpublished HEC/NUST basis). Punjab EPA publicly rejected PAQI's local-emissions framing and attributes pollution to cross-border factors.
5. Islamabad has exactly one quantitative apportionment (PAQI 2025). Everything else is Pak-EPA qualitative statements.

Blocked/unverified: Express Tribune, Profit, Dialogue Earth, NPR, ScienceDirect/Springer abstracts returned 403; the WB "Towards Clean Air for Punjab" advisory report itself is not public (its numbers come via the PID/ESSA); the HEC/NUST 30% study is not published.
