Lahore got a trace of rain, Faisalabad and Multan none. Their air improved anyway

By Hammad Tariq

Rain came to northern Pakistan between 10 and 14 November, and most of it stayed in the hills. Murree's rain gauge collected at least {{rain_pk.murree_total_11_15}} mm. Lahore's recorded a trace, too little to measure, and the gauges at Faisalabad, Multan and Bahawalpur recorded nothing. On the Indian side, the India Meteorological Department's rainfall total for 1 to 15 November rounds to zero in {{rain_in.punjab_zero}} of the {{rain_in.punjab_districts_listed}} Punjab districts it lists. Yet the air in the cities of Pakistani Punjab improved sharply on the night of 12 to 13 November. IQAir's figure for Lahore fell from an air quality index (AQI) of {{lahore_night.at_2000}} at 8 pm to {{lahore_night.at_0500}} at 5 am, and stayed below 200 for {{cities.lahore.below_200_hours}} hours. That night was the windiest of the six nights from 9 to 15 November at the airports of Lahore, Faisalabad, Multan, Sialkot and Amritsar. By 8 pm on 13 November Lahore was back above 200. On 14 and 15 November it averaged {{cities.lahore.day.14.mean}} and {{cities.lahore.day.15.mean}}: lower than on 10 November, the last full day we captured before it, and still in the range the US index calls very unhealthy.

The rain was heaviest in the hills: {{rain_pk.stations.Murree.2026-11-13|1}} mm at Murree and {{rain_pk.stations.Balakot.2026-11-13|1}} mm at Balakot in the 24 hours to 8 am on 13 November. In Lahore, the city observatory's report for 5 am on 13 November recorded a trace of rain in the previous three hours, the only rain in any of its reports from 10 November to the morning of 15 November. Its 24-hour figure is missing from that morning's report. Lahore airport reported rain falling once, at 3 am the same night, and at no other time from 9 November to midday on 15 November. Faisalabad, Multan and Bahawalpur recorded 0.0 mm on every one of the five days, and the airports at Faisalabad and Multan did not report rain at all. Sargodha's reports were empty, so we have no figure for it.

On the Indian side, the India Meteorological Department's district table for Punjab, issued on 15 November, gives the rainfall for 1 to 15 November as zero in {{rain_in.punjab_zero}} of the {{rain_in.punjab_districts_listed}} districts listed, and 0.1 mm in Pathankot. Its Haryana table shows zero in all {{rain_in.haryana_rows_listed}} rows, which include Chandigarh. The figures are rounded to a tenth of a millimetre: the same bulletin says very light rain fell at isolated places in Punjab on 14 to 15 November. In Delhi, four of the five stations had no rain or a trace for the month to 15 November, and Ayanagar had 1.4 mm.

Lahore's night, hour by hour

IQAir reports Lahore's air as one city figure, the median of the stations reporting in each hour. In the hour from 8 pm on 12 November it stood at {{lahore_night.at_2000}}, higher than at the same hour on 10 November ({{lahore_night.at_2000_10}}). It then fell through the evening, to {{lahore_night.at_0100_13}} in the hour from 1 am and {{lahore_night.at_0200}} from 2 am. In those hours Lahore airport reported smoke or mist, visibility of {{lahore_airport.vis_m_2000|,}} m, a wind of {{lahore_airport.wind_kmh_20_to_0230_min}} to {{lahore_airport.wind_kmh_20_to_0230_max}} km/h and no rain.

At 3 am the airport made its only report of rain falling. The city figure was {{lahore_night.at_0300}} in that hour, {{lahore_night.at_0400}} in the next, and {{lahore_night.at_0500}} in the hour from 5 am. The wind at the airport rose to {{lahore_airport.wind_kmh_0500}} km/h at 5 am and {{lahore_airport.wind_peak_kmh}} km/h at 5:30 am, from the north-north-east. Visibility improved to {{lahore_airport.vis_m_0430|,}} m at 4:30 am and {{lahore_airport.vis_m_0700|,}} m at 7 am.

So the figure had already fallen {{lahore_night.fall_2000_to_0100}} points by the hour from 1 am, before any rain was reported. The other {{lahore_night.fall_0100_to_0500}} points came between then and 5 am, the hours in which the trace of rain fell and the wind picked up. The fall was city-wide: of Lahore's {{lahore_night.stations_in_capture}} stations, {{lahore_night.stations_with_both_hours}} had a reading in both hours, and every one of them was lower at 5 am than at 8 pm, by at least half.

The three nights before 12 November were close to calm on the plains: the airports at Lahore, Faisalabad and Multan reported average winds of {{calm_nights.min_kmh}} to {{calm_nights.max_kmh}} km/h. On the night of 12 to 13 November the average was {{wind_nights.Lahore.12-13.mean_kmh}} km/h at Lahore, {{wind_nights.Faisalabad.12-13.mean_kmh}} at Faisalabad, {{wind_nights.Multan.12-13.mean_kmh}} at Multan, {{wind_nights.Sialkot.12-13.mean_kmh}} at Sialkot and {{wind_nights.Amritsar.12-13.mean_kmh}} at Amritsar, the highest of the six nights at each of the five. These are still light winds. Islamabad's airport, by contrast, had {{wind_nights.Islamabad.11-12.mean_kmh}} to {{wind_nights.Islamabad.13-14.mean_kmh}} km/h on every one of the six nights.

Moving air spreads smoke out and carries it away, and a weather system of this kind can also change how deep a layer the smoke mixes into. We did not measure that depth, and an airport report describes one place at one moment. What the gauges do show is that no measurable rain fell at the Lahore, Faisalabad and Multan observatories, so rain falling on these cities is unlikely to be what improved their air. They cannot exclude cleaner air arriving on the wind from places where it did rain.

Fires

On the NOAA-20 satellite, the two Punjabs averaged {{fires.noaa20_mean_7_10}} fire spots a day from 7 to 10 November. The count was {{fires.days.2026-11-11.two_punjabs_noaa20}} on 11 November, {{fires.days.2026-11-12.two_punjabs_noaa20}} on the 12th and {{fires.days.2026-11-13.two_punjabs_noaa20}} on the 13th, the two lowest days of the month so far, then {{fires.days.2026-11-14.two_punjabs_noaa20}} on the 14th and {{fires.days.2026-11-15.two_punjabs_noaa20}} on the 15th. Across all satellites the counts for the five days from 11 to 15 November were {{fires.days.2026-11-11.two_punjabs|,}}, {{fires.days.2026-11-12.two_punjabs}}, {{fires.days.2026-11-13.two_punjabs}}, {{fires.days.2026-11-14.two_punjabs}} and {{fires.days.2026-11-15.two_punjabs|,}}. One satellite's count also depends on where its passes fall each day.

Satellites cannot see fires through cloud. Lahore's observatory, the only one whose cloud reports we checked, reported {{rain_pk.lahore.cloud_min_12nov1400_to_13nov1400}} to {{rain_pk.lahore.cloud_max_12nov1400_to_13nov1400}} eighths of the sky covered from 2 pm on 12 November to 2 pm on 13 November, after clear skies on the two days before. So the low counts do not show that burning stopped, and since the NOAA-20 count was already lower on 11 November under a clear sky at Lahore, cloud may not explain all of the drop either. If farmers did burn less during the spell, that would also have helped the air; these data cannot separate it from the wind.

What the data can and cannot tell us

  • What was measured. Rain of {{rain_pk.stations.Murree.2026-11-13|1}} mm in a day at Murree, {{rain_pk.stations.Sialkot City.2026-11-13|1}} mm at Sialkot, a trace at Lahore and none at Faisalabad, Multan and Bahawalpur. A fall in IQAir's Lahore figure from {{lahore_night.at_2000}} to {{lahore_night.at_0500}} in nine hours, with falls in the other cities of Pakistani Punjab the same night. The windiest of six nights at five airports.
  • What that makes unlikely. That rain falling on Lahore, Faisalabad or Multan washed their air clean. Air already cleaned by rain further north may still have reached them on the wind.
  • What it cannot separate. How much of the improvement came from the wind, from deeper mixing of the air, or from fewer fires under the cloud.
  • The gap. We have no hourly air readings from 1 am on 11 November to 7 pm on 12 November, so we cannot say how the air changed on 11 and 12 November, when the first rain fell in the north.
  • The Indian side. The rainfall record is clear: effectively none in Punjab and Haryana. The air record is not, because IQAir shows one relayed government feed per city.

Method and sources

  • Rainfall, Pakistan: the Pakistan Meteorological Department's observatory reports (SYNOP) as exchanged between weather services and relayed by OGIMET, fetched on 15 November. The 24-hour total is the figure in the 03:00 UTC (8 am) report. We decoded the reports with a script; the rules and the raw reports are in the repository. We could not open the department's own rainfall pages on 14 and 15 November.
  • Rainfall, India: India Meteorological Department, Meteorological Centre Chandigarh, "Rainfall Status" bulletins for Punjab and Haryana, and the New Delhi centre's observation table, all issued on 15 November. The bulletins of 12 to 14 November had been replaced before we fetched them, so we use the month-to-date column.
  • Wind, weather and visibility: airport weather reports (METAR) for Lahore, Faisalabad, Multan, Sialkot, Islamabad and Amritsar from the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive, 9 November to midday on 15 November. Night averages use every report from 8 pm up to, but not including, 8 am, counting calm as zero. Knots are converted to km/h. We also fetched the reports for Delhi and Chandigarh airports and do not use them, because this report is about the two Punjabs.
  • Air quality: IQAir city and station pages, History, Hourly, US AQI⁺, captured at 1:30 am on 11 November, 8 pm on 14 November and 12:30 am on 16 November. IQAir keeps about 47 hours of hourly history, so the hours from 1 am on 11 November to 7 pm on 12 November were not captured. The city figure is the median of the stations reporting in an hour (IQAir data computation). Hours are given by their start. Where two captures overlap we use the later one, because IQAir revises hours.
  • Fires: NASA FIRMS VIIRS and MODIS detections merged by HamariHawa's daily archive (hamarihawa.org/api/day), retrieved on 16 November; near-real-time data, counted per Pakistan-time day. The latest days can still change as late data arrive.
  • Weather models: none of the rainfall or wind figures here comes from a model.
