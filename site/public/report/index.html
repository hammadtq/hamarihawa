Lahore & Islamabad, two years of air

A side-by-side ledger of what people in both cities actually breathed over the last 24 months, where the pollution comes from, and what the published projections say about the years ahead.

Primary series IQAir ground sensors (61 stations in Lahore, 11 in Islamabad) Pollutant profile Copernicus CAMS reanalysis via Open-Meteo Compiled 14 Sep 2026
01 · The ledger

Same country, two different airsheds

Lahore sits in the Indo-Gangetic Plain smog basin; Islamabad sits 500 m higher against the Margalla Hills. Over the same 24 months Lahore averaged roughly twice Islamabad's fine-particle load, and its smog season runs longer and peaks harder.

02 · Monthly PM2.5

The winter spike is the whole story

Both cities are moderate-to-poor from March to September, then step up together in October. Lahore's step is two to three times taller: November 2024 averaged 286 µg/m³, a typical-day AQI of 336, the worst month either city recorded. The one month Islamabad came close was December 2025, when it hit 122 µg/m³ against Lahore's 171.

05 · What contributes

Where the pollution comes from

Source apportionment for Lahore has been studied repeatedly; Islamabad has no dedicated apportionment study, so its picture is assembled from emissions inventories, Pak-EPA statements and regional studies. Percentages differ by study because they measure different things (emission tonnage vs. ambient PM2.5 contribution) in different seasons.

What Pak-EPA names, without percentages

Pak-EPA statements, Nov 2024 – Oct 2025

Vehicles and road dust; non-zigzag brick kilns on the outskirts (demolished in sectors H-16 and H-17 after an October 2025 deadline); steel units; round-the-clock construction machinery on Blue Area high-rises; pollution drifting in from Rawalpindi; CDA burning garbage in public bins. PM2.5 makes up about 90% of the pollution load. December 2024 was the worst month on record, with a daily AQI peak of 250.

What the receptor studies add

Academic sampling, 2012 and 2023

Source fingerprinting in the industrial area (2023 samples, EPA-PMF) finds steel mills, marble processing, e-waste incineration, battery processing and vehicles, with higher PM in cooler months from thermal inversions in the Margalla basin. No study quantifies Margalla forest fires or cross-border transport for the capital.

Journal of Trace Elements and Minerals, 2025 · PIXE study, Nilore, 2012

Why winter is worse in both cities

Seasonal meteorology

From late October a temperature inversion sits at 700–900 hPa over the Indo-Gangetic Plain, the mixing layer collapses to about 300 m, winds drop to 0–3 mph and humidity climbs past 75%, so smoke nucleates fog. Rice-residue burning in both Punjabs is confined to October–November, which is why it matters more to smog than its annual tonnage suggests. Islamabad's basin, ringed by the Margalla Hills, traps its own air in autumn and spring.

Frontiers in Sustainable Cities, 2023 · Environ. Sci. Pollut. Res., 2026

How much comes from India? Estimates for Lahore run from 13% (World Bank, Punjab-wide, 2021) to 17% of winter aerosol point sources (FAO, 2019) to 30% (Punjab government, citing an unpublished HEC/NUST study for Oct–Nov 2023). No peer-reviewed apportionment of the cross-border share exists. The World Bank's airshed work finds the flow is two-way: about 30% of Indian Punjab's pollution originates in Pakistani Punjab under the prevailing north-west winds.
06 · Looking ahead

Projections that exist, and the ones that don't

No published model projects a monthly or annual AQI number for Lahore or Islamabad specifically. What exists are policy targets, regional scenario models, and trend readings. They are listed with their horizon and what they actually promise.

Lahore Division2030model

Business as usual: 87 → 99.8 µg/m³. With the $300M Punjab Clean Air Program: 97.5, and about 6 µg/m³ lower by 2035.

The only model-based, city-level projection that exists. The World Bank labels it provisional, to be revised once Punjab's emissions inventory is built. The program funds 600 electric buses, 5,000 super-seeders for rice stubble, kiln conversions and monitoring, and closes in June 2030. The "35% cut in a decade" figure in press coverage is a Pakistan-wide World Bank target, not a Lahore projection.

Program Appraisal Document, Mar 2025 · press release

Lahore2030policy target

30% below the 2022 annual PM2.5 in Lahore, Faisalabad, Gujranwala and Multan.

Punjab Clean Air Policy, notified April 2023. Against IQAir's 2022 value of 97.4 µg/m³ that implies about 68 µg/m³ in 2030, still 13 times the WHO guideline. It is a target with no modelling behind it. The same policy promises all brick kilns on zigzag technology by 2030 and a 25% cut in NOx, SOx, ozone and CO from 2021 levels.

Punjab Clean Air Policy 2023

Punjab2030scenarios

Population-weighted PM2.5 of 48.8 µg/m³ if 2018 laws are fully enforced; 41.9 with ad-hoc measures; 28.6 with cost-optimal airshed cooperation; 17.3 at the technical maximum.

World Bank and IIASA GAINS modelling for the "Punjab (Pakistan)" region, the closest scenario set for both cities. About 40% of the achievable reduction in the Indo-Gangetic Plain depends on controls outside one's own region, which is the case for treating the two Punjabs, Haryana and Delhi as one airshed. The Bank's later program document puts Punjab on a rising path instead: 52 today to 58 by 2030 without action.

Striving for Clean Air, 2023, Tables A.4.1 and A.4.3 · Purohit et al., 2024

Pakistan2035target

"35 by 35": national population-weighted PM2.5 from 55 to 35 µg/m³ by 2035, the WHO interim target 1.

Adopted in the World Bank Country Partnership Framework for 2026–35 and echoed in the National Clean Air Policy, which targets PM2.5 emissions 38% below baseline in 2030 and 81% below in 2040. SEI's modelling of that policy's 18 measures finds a 36–39% emissions cut by 2030 if implemented. A September 2026 review estimates full implementation would avert about 129,500 deaths a year by 2040.

National Clean Air Policy 2023 · Slater et al., SEI 2024 · Dawn, 8 Sep 2026

Pakistan2050model

Under current legislation PM2.5 rises about 1.5× by 2050; under stringent controls it halves.

IIASA GAINS modelling for Pakistan as a whole. A separate 2025 disease-burden model finds PM2.5 rising 0.2–0.5 µg/m³ a year over four decades and PM2.5-attributable deaths passing 290,000 a year by 2030 on the current path, versus about 29,000 under mitigation.

Mir et al., Environmental Science & Policy 2022 · Khan et al., Environmental Pollution 2025

Indo-Gangetic Plain2025–49climate

Every climate-meteorology study points to more winter stagnation, so the same emissions will produce worse smog.

A 2026 five-model CMIP6 ensemble focused on Lahore finds increasingly persistent 700–900 hPa inversions and longer stagnation windows through 2049 under SSP5-8.5. Regional work agrees: no emissions pathway improves South Asian air by 2050 (Kumar et al. 2018); SSP3-7.0 adds about 8 µg/m³ to South Asian PM2.5 by 2050 (Xu et al. 2022); each extra winter stagnation day in the plain adds roughly 1 µg/m³ (Zhou et al. 2024). None of these give a Lahore or Islamabad number.

Ghani et al., 2026 · Kumar et al., 2018 · Xu et al., 2022 · Zhou et al., 2024

Islamabadnone

No projection and no numeric target exists for the capital.

Pak-EPA's Vehicular Emission Control Action Plan (Oct 2025) sets Euro-5 fuel by 2027 and Euro-6 by 2030 with no PM2.5 or AQI goal. The CDA and Asian Development Bank scoped an Air Quality Improvement programme in December 2025, with no figures yet. The nearest proxies are the Punjab scenarios above and the Air Quality Life Index reading: at 51 µg/m³ (2023) Islamabad residents lose 4.5 years of life expectancy against the WHO guideline, Lahore Division residents 5.8.

APP, 19 Oct 2025 · Dawn, Dec 2025 · AQLI Pakistan fact sheet 2025

Both cities2026–27season

No quantitative forecast for the coming smog season exists from Punjab EPA, PMD, UNEP or Copernicus.

Punjab launched a pre-emptive anti-smog drive on 3 September 2026 without a severity outlook; CAMS issues only five-day forecasts. The 2025 season was markedly better than 2024 (Lahore's November average fell 37%, the daily peak from 539 to 237 µg/m³, and no day above AQI 300), but PAQI attributes much of that to favourable weather and warns it can reverse. The direction of the 24-month series is consistent with that: Lahore's Jan–Aug 2026 mean of 70 µg/m³ sits between its 2025 (68) and 2024 (75) figures for the same months.

07 · Method and sources

How the numbers were assembled

Ground data (primary)

  • Jan–Dec 2025: monthly PM2.5 from IQAir's 2025 World Air Quality Report city table (iqair.com/world-most-polluted-cities, data stamped 31 Mar 2026). Lahore ranked 8th most polluted city in the world for 2025 at 88.9 µg/m³; Islamabad ranked 92nd at 48.1 µg/m³.
  • Jan–Sep 2026: monthly means from the Historic air quality panel on IQAir's Lahore and Islamabad city pages, read on 14 Sep 2026. September 2026 is a partial month (1–14 Sep).
  • Sep–Dec 2024: monthly PM2.5 from the Pakistan city table in IQAir's 2024 World Air Quality Report (PDF, mirrored by Greenpeace Chile; IQAir's own site gates the file behind a form). Its 2024 annual values (Lahore 102.1, Islamabad 52.4) match the IQAir website. Islamabad was the 5th most polluted capital in 2024.
  • IQAir aggregates US consulate and embassy monitors, Punjab EPA and Pak-EPA stations, The Urban Unit, Pakistan Air Quality Initiative and low-cost community sensors: 61 stations from 33 contributors in Lahore, 11 stations from 9 contributors in Islamabad.

Model data and caveats

  • Hourly PM2.5, PM10, NO₂, SO₂, O₃, CO and dust for 1 Sep 2024 – 13 Sep 2026 from the Open-Meteo Air Quality API (Copernicus CAMS global reanalysis and forecast, ~11 km grid), averaged to months.
  • CAMS under-reads the ground network in winter by a wide margin: Oct–Feb IQAir/CAMS ratio averages 1.5 in Lahore and 1.6 in Islamabad, and CAMS over-reads Islamabad in summer (ratio 0.8). That is why the model series is used only for the pollutant mix, never for the headline numbers.
  • US AQI per month is derived from the monthly mean PM2.5 with the 2024 EPA breakpoints (12 / 35.4 / 55.4 / 150.4 / 250.4 µg/m³). IQAir's own monthly AQI figures for Oct 2025 – Sep 2026 matched this derivation within 1–2 points.
  • aqi.in, a second aggregator with its own sensors, reads Lahore's 2025 annual AQI as 142 and 2026 to date as 116, consistent in direction with IQAir (2025 worse than 2026 so far).
