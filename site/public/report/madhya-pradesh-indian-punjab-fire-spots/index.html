Madhya Pradesh overtakes Indian Punjab: satellite fire spots in October, 2019–2026

By Hammad Tariq

From 1 to 24 October 2026 the Suomi NPP satellite recorded 1,831 fire spots in Madhya Pradesh, the most in eight years. Indian Punjab had 453 and Haryana 62, the fewest in eight years. These are satellite heat detections, not confirmed fires. The crossing is not new: in the late season, 25 October to 30 November, Madhya Pradesh already had more spots than Indian Punjab in 2024 (13,229 against 6,912) and 2025 (13,876 against 2,954). India's national count of 12,600, +0.3% on the 2019–2025 mean, looks steady mainly because coal and industrial districts lead it, not because the burning moved.

Two records in the same three weeks

All year-to-year counts here are fire spots seen by one satellite, Suomi NPP, between 1 and 24 October. A fire spot is a heat detection, not confirmed fires on the ground, and the label 'crop' is seasonal, not a confirmed field burn.

Madhya Pradesh had 1,831 spots in 2026, the highest of eight years and +173.7% on its 2019–2025 mean of 669.0; in 2019 it had 131. Indian Punjab had 453, the lowest of eight years and −89.2% on a mean of 4,185.3; in 2020 it had 10,842. Haryana had 62, also the lowest, −95.6% on a mean of 1,398.9; in 2019 it had 2,615.

Rajasthan had 622, the second highest of eight years and +60.7% on a mean of 387.1. India as a whole had 12,600, fourth of eight years and +0.3% on a mean of 12,564.4. Indian Punjab's count has fallen in every year since 2021, while Madhya Pradesh's earlier high was 1,304 in 2023.

The two movements are not a mirror image. Madhya Pradesh's rise is much smaller, in spots, than Indian Punjab's fall, so it cannot on its own hold India's total steady. The states also differ in size and cropped area: these are counts, not densities, so each state is best read against its own past.

Madhya Pradesh passed Indian Punjab in the late season in 2024

The first 24 days of October are only the opening of the season. From 25 October to 30 November, Indian Punjab's Suomi NPP count fell from 64,893 in 2021 to 6,912 in 2024 and 2,954 in 2025, the lowest of seven years and −92.2% on the earlier mean of 38,036.8.

Over the same weeks Madhya Pradesh had 13,229 spots in 2024 and 13,876 in 2025, its highest of seven years and +46.8% on its earlier mean of 9,455.0. So Madhya Pradesh has had more late-season spots than Indian Punjab for at least two seasons; the 2026 October record is the early-window sign of a crossing that had already happened.

Madhya Pradesh's late season is much bigger than its October count. Whether 2026 brings a bigger season or only an earlier start is not yet known; that will be clear only after 30 November.

Guna and Ashoknagar lead, as they have before

Counting all satellites, Guna was third among Indian districts from 1 to 24 October 2026 with 1,133 spots (Suomi NPP 346) and Ashoknagar fifth with 724 (242). Sehore (403; 163) and Shivpuri (387; 103) were also in the top 20.

These are not new places. In 2023 Guna led Madhya Pradesh with 768 spots (Suomi NPP 255), 20.5% of the state, with Narmadapuram, Ashoknagar and Shivpuri also in the top six. In 2025 Guna was second with 197 (Suomi NPP 69), behind Singrauli with 217 (102).

In Guna, daily counts were in single digits in early October, jumped to 111 on 18 October and peaked at 115 on 22 October.

In the week of 18–24 October, Guna led India with 657 spots (+59.5% on the week before) and Ashoknagar was third with 467 (+106.6%). Shivpuri (262), Narmadapuram (220) and Bundi in Rajasthan (255, +672.7%) entered the top 12. Indian Punjab's season is starting too: Tarn Taran had 456 (+171.4%) and Amritsar 245 (+210.1%). HamariHawa's live feed for the 24 hours to the satellite pass of 25 October still listed Guna (105) and Ashoknagar (72) among the busiest districts, though that falls outside the window counted here.

Did the burning move? India's total is held up by industrial heat

The strongest alternative reading of a 'shift' is that burning moved from Indian Punjab and Haryana to Madhya Pradesh, keeping India's total level. The district lists do not support that. The two busiest Indian districts from 1 to 24 October were Dhanbad in Jharkhand (1,641 spots; Suomi NPP 838) and Angul in Odisha (1,438; 810), and Paschim Bardhaman, Bokaro, Raigarh, Jharsuguda, Jajpur, Sundargarh, Ballari, Korba and Ramgarh are also in the top 20.

Dhanbad has 123 known pollution sources within 25 km of its fire centre: 120 places where fires are detected on many days a year and 3 power plants, one detected on 201 days. On 24 October the busiest villages included Dera Colliery Township in Angul (27) and Kusunda in Dhanbad (23). A large part of India's steady total appears to be industrial heat, not crop burning.

Madhya Pradesh's leading districts look different. There are no known industrial sites, kilns or landfills within 30 km of the centre of Guna district's fire spots on 23 or 24 October, or of the centre of Ashoknagar district's fire spots on 24 October. That fits fires on farmland, but the sites directory may be incomplete, and Singrauli, which led the state in 2025 and is known as a coal and power district, was not checked.

Both things are true at once: Indian Punjab and Haryana have fallen, and Madhya Pradesh has risen. Nothing in these counts shows that one caused the other. Madhya Pradesh grows different crops on a different harvest calendar, so its fires may well not be paddy residue at all.

Fewer fires, or fires the satellite missed?

Suomi NPP passes over in the early afternoon, so burning later in the day, or in smaller patches, could escape it. HamariHawa's archive gives night detections for the whole region, not per state, so this cannot be fully tested for Indian Punjab or Haryana.

A partial check is the all-satellite count, which includes other passes. Indian Punjab had 1,609 all-satellite spots in 2026, against 1,626 in 2025 and 22,208 in 2020; Haryana had 222. All-satellite counts are not comparable across years and would tend to rise as satellites are added, so a low figure weakens, but does not rule out, the idea that farmers are burning later or in smaller patches.

Can these counts be trusted?

No days are flagged missing, but 13 October 2026 is effectively a Suomi NPP gap: India had 4 Suomi NPP spots that day, against 337 to 1,047 on every other day, and Guna had 0 against 31 from all satellites. The gap affects only 2026, so it slightly undercounts this year: Madhya Pradesh's record is conservative and Indian Punjab's low slightly deeper.

State totals are built from each day's list of busiest districts, so they slightly undercount. Counts are not normalised for cropped area, and none of these spots has been checked on the ground.

What is not known: whether Madhya Pradesh's 2026 season will be bigger or only earlier; how much of the state total comes from industrial sources such as Singrauli; and which cities smoke from central India reaches. These counts cannot answer the last question.

How this was counted

  • Data: NASA FIRMS satellite heat detections (VIIRS on Suomi NPP, NOAA-20 and NOAA-21; MODIS on Aqua and Terra), assigned to days in Pakistan time.
  • One satellite: every comparison between years uses Suomi NPP only. All-satellite counts appear only for within-2026 district rankings and weekly changes, and as a partial check; they are not comparable across years.
  • Fire spots: a spot is a heat detection, not confirmed fires. 'Crop' is a seasonal label, not a confirmed field burn.
  • Places: Indian states and districts. Counts are not densities and are not normalised for cropped area, so states are compared mainly with their own past.
  • Windows: 1–24 October for 2019–2026; 25 October–30 November for 2019–2025.
  • State totals: built from each day's list of busiest districts, so they slightly undercount.
  • District histories: a day is listed as absent, not zero, when the district was not in that day's busiest list (Guna on 1, 2 and 4 October 2026).
  • Missing days: none flagged, but 13 October 2026 is effectively a Suomi NPP gap (4 spots across India); it affects 2026 only.
  • Source check: known industrial sites, kilns, landfills and recurring hotspots within 30 km of the Guna and Ashoknagar fire centres and 25 km of the Dhanbad centre, from HamariHawa's sites directory.
  • Limits: no per-state time-of-day data; the sites directory may miss sources; Singrauli not checked; the 2026 season is unfinished; no ground-truthing.

मध्य प्रदेश में 2026 में 1,831 जगहें दर्ज हुईं, जो आठ साल में सबसे ज़्यादा हैं और 2019–2025 के उसके औसत 669.0 से +173.7% हैं; 2019 में यहाँ 131 जगहें थीं। भारतीय पंजाब में 453 जगहें रहीं, जो आठ साल में सबसे कम हैं और 4,185.3 के औसत से −89.2% हैं; 2020 में यहाँ 10,842 जगहें थीं। हरियाणा में 62 जगहें रहीं, यह भी सबसे कम, 1,398.9 के औसत से −95.6%; 2019 में यहाँ 2,615 जगहें थीं।

राजस्थान में 622 जगहें रहीं, जो आठ साल में दूसरी सबसे ज़्यादा हैं और 387.1 के औसत से +60.7% हैं। पूरे भारत में 12,600 जगहें रहीं, जो आठ साल में चौथे स्थान पर हैं और 12,564.4 के औसत से +0.3% हैं। भारतीय पंजाब की गिनती 2021 के बाद हर साल घटी है, जबकि मध्य प्रदेश का पिछला सबसे ऊँचा आँकड़ा 2023 में 1,304 था।

आग कम हुई, या उपग्रह से छूट गई?

Suomi NPP दोपहर की शुरुआत में ऊपर से गुज़रता है, इसलिए दिन में बाद में या छोटे टुकड़ों में जलाई गई आग उससे छूट सकती है। HamariHawa के संग्रह में रात की पकड़ पूरे क्षेत्र के लिए है, हर राज्य के लिए अलग नहीं, इसलिए भारतीय पंजाब या हरियाणा के लिए इसकी पूरी जाँच नहीं हो सकती।

आंशिक जाँच सभी उपग्रहों की गिनती से होती है, जिसमें दूसरे उपग्रहों के चक्कर भी शामिल हैं। भारतीय पंजाब में 2026 में सभी उपग्रहों से 1,609 जगहें दर्ज हुईं, जबकि 2025 में 1,626 और 2020 में 22,208 थीं; हरियाणा में 222 रहीं। सभी उपग्रहों की गिनती को सालों के बीच नहीं मिलाया जा सकता और उपग्रह जुड़ने के साथ यह बढ़ने की ओर झुकती है, इसलिए कम आँकड़ा इस धारणा को कमज़ोर करता है, लेकिन ख़ारिज नहीं करता, कि किसान बाद में या छोटे टुकड़ों में जला रहे हैं।

राज्यों का कुल हर दिन के सबसे व्यस्त ज़िलों की सूची से बनाया जाता है, इसलिए यह थोड़ा कम गिनता है। गिनतियों को खेती वाले रकबे के हिसाब से समायोजित नहीं किया गया है, और इनमें से किसी भी जगह की ज़मीन पर जाँच नहीं हुई है।

क्या पता नहीं है: मध्य प्रदेश का 2026 का सीज़न बड़ा होगा या बस जल्दी शुरू हुआ है; राज्य के कुल आँकड़े का कितना हिस्सा सिंगरौली जैसे औद्योगिक स्रोतों से आता है; और मध्य भारत का धुआँ किन शहरों तक पहुँचता है। ये गिनतियाँ आख़िरी सवाल का जवाब नहीं दे सकतीं।

ये आँकड़े कैसे गिने गए

  • आँकड़े: नासा FIRMS से उपग्रह द्वारा पकड़ी गई गर्मी (Suomi NPP, NOAA-20 और NOAA-21 पर VIIRS; Aqua और Terra पर MODIS), जिन्हें पाकिस्तान के समय के हिसाब से दिनों में बाँटा गया है।
  • एक उपग्रह: सालों के बीच की हर तुलना सिर्फ़ Suomi NPP से की गई है। सभी उपग्रहों की गिनती सिर्फ़ 2026 के भीतर ज़िलों के क्रम और साप्ताहिक बदलाव के लिए, और आंशिक जाँच के तौर पर, दी गई है; इसे सालों के बीच नहीं मिलाया जा सकता।
  • आग की जगहें: एक जगह का मतलब उपग्रह से पकड़ी गई गर्मी है, पुष्टि की गई आग नहीं। 'फ़सल' मौसम के आधार पर लगाया गया लेबल है, खेत में जलाने की पुष्टि नहीं।
  • इलाके: भारत के राज्य और ज़िले। गिनतियाँ घनत्व नहीं हैं और खेती वाले रकबे के हिसाब से समायोजित नहीं की गई हैं, इसलिए राज्यों की तुलना मुख्य रूप से उनके अपने पिछले आँकड़ों से की गई है।
  • समय-सीमा: 2019–2026 के लिए 1–24 अक्टूबर; 2019–2025 के लिए 25 अक्टूबर–30 नवंबर।
  • राज्यों का कुल: हर दिन के सबसे व्यस्त ज़िलों की सूची से बनाया गया, इसलिए यह थोड़ा कम गिनता है।
  • ज़िलों का इतिहास: जिस दिन ज़िला सबसे व्यस्त ज़िलों की सूची में नहीं था, उस दिन को शून्य नहीं, बल्कि अनुपस्थित दिखाया गया है (गुना में 1, 2 और 4 अक्टूबर 2026)।
  • छूटे दिन: किसी दिन को छूटा हुआ नहीं बताया गया, लेकिन 13 अक्टूबर 2026 असल में Suomi NPP का ख़ाली दिन है (पूरे भारत में 4 जगहें); इसका असर सिर्फ़ 2026 पर है।
  • स्रोत की जाँच: गुना और अशोकनगर की आग के केंद्रों से 30 किमी और धनबाद के केंद्र से 25 किमी के भीतर ज्ञात औद्योगिक स्थल, भट्ठे, कचरा डंप और बार-बार आग वाली जगहें, HamariHawa की स्थल-सूची से।
  • सीमाएँ: हर राज्य के लिए दिन के समय का अलग डेटा नहीं; स्थल-सूची से कुछ स्रोत छूट सकते हैं; सिंगरौली की जाँच नहीं हुई; 2026 का सीज़न अभी पूरा नहीं हुआ; ज़मीन पर कोई पुष्टि नहीं।
